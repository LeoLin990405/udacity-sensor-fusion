Summary:
- P supplies immediate correction, I removes persistent bias, and D damps
  abrupt changes and oscillation.
- P, PI, and PID gain sets were compared independently for throttle and steer.
- Automatic tuning can minimize RMS tracking error, command jerk, saturation,
  and safety penalties over repeated CARLA episodes.
- PID is inexpensive, interpretable, and model-free, but needs retuning and
  cannot anticipate constraints. Model-based control can optimize a horizon and
  couple actuators, but costs more and is sensitive to model mismatch.
- Integral anti-windup is implemented. Gain scheduling, derivative filtering,
  and curvature feed-forward are proposed next improvements.
